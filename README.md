# Geo-Dev-Lab

A hands-on laboratory for building practical skills in **GIS, geospatial data, Python, web GIS, software development, AI, and agentic systems**. The programme is structured into two phases, progressing from GIS foundations to the development of intelligent geospatial applications.

## Phase One: GIS, Geospatial Programming and Web GIS

* **Month 1:** GIS foundations and the geospatial data ecosystem · **Weeks 1–4**
* **Month 2:** Development environment and Python foundations · **Weeks 5–9**
* **Month 3:** Geospatial Python and automation · **Weeks 10–14**
* **Month 4:** Web foundations and Web GIS · **Weeks 15–19**
* **Month 5:** Backend, databases and shipping Phase One · **Weeks 20–25**
* **Month 6:** Consolidation and Phase One review · **Weeks 26–27**

### Month 1

**Week 1:** Choosing a question the data can answer
Exploring real-world problems, developing spatial questions, and identifying the data needed to answer them.
</br>
**Task:** A major real-world spatial problem relating to the distribution of healthcare facilities in Lagos State was identified and used to develop the main spatial question for the project. The datasets required to answer the question were identified, and potential data sources were explored and documented. A proposed methodology was developed to guide the analysis, and the expected outputs were defined. <br />
**Project:** [View Week 1](./Week%201/) 

**Week 2:** Understanding spatial data and getting yours
Learning what spatial data is, understanding vector and raster data, finding reliable datasets, and acquiring the data needed for analysis. </br?
**Task:** The datasets required for the analysis were acquired and documented. These included the Lagos Local Government Area (LGA) boundaries and Lagos State administrative boundary obtained from the Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX), healthcare facility data from the Lagos State Health Facility Assessment Datasets – HSCL, and LGA population and demographic data, including population estimates from the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) accessed through Kaggle. The datasets were reviewed and prepared for subsequent spatial analysis. 
</br>
**Project:** [View Week 2](./Week%202/) 

**Week 3:** Coordinate systems and preparing your data
Understanding coordinate reference systems, projections, datums, EPSG codes, and preparing spatial data for analysis.
</br>
**Task:** The coordinate reference systems of the spatial datasets were reviewed, and the data were transformed from the WGS 84 geographic coordinate system to the WGS 84 / UTM Zone 31N projected coordinate system to support spatial analysis. Data quality checks were also conducted using five key criteria: completeness, currency, positional accuracy, attribute accuracy, and fitness for purpose. The identified data quality issues and limitations were documented, and the suitability of the datasets for answering the spatial question was assessed. <br/>
**Project:** [View Week 3](./week%203/) 

**Week 4:** Spatial relationships and analysis
Exploring spatial relationships, spatial joins, proximity, buffers, overlays, intersections, and other fundamental spatial analysis techniques.
</br>
**Task:** A spatial join was performed to determine the number of healthcare facilities within each of the 20 Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Lagos State. The population-to-healthcare-facility ratio was then calculated for each LGA and used to classify the areas according to their relative level of healthcare facility availability in relation to population. A map was produced to visualize the different levels across Lagos State, and the results were interpreted to answer the main spatial question. The analysis also identified limitations and areas for future exploration, particularly the need to investigate physical accessibility to healthcare facilities. </br>
**Project:** [View Week 4](./Week%204/)
